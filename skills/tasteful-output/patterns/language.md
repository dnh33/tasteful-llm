# Language patterns, L1 through L17

17 patterns where word choice creates slop, grouped by root disease. The examples are illustrative, and other wordings of the same move count.

## Modifier diseases (L1, L3, L6, L13, L14, L16)

Adjectives pile up, synonyms stack, triples fill space, era-marked vocabulary clusters, significance clauses get bolted on, and enthusiasm stands in for information.

- L1 Adjective Crutch: stacking modifiers where one fact would do
- L3 Thesaurus Parade: three synonyms in a row that all mean the same thing, and the tricolon reflex (three items where the content has one or two)
- L6 Enthusiasm Proxy: your excitement replacing the reader's interest
- L13 Era Vocabulary: "delve", "tapestry", "pivotal", "underscore", flagged at more than 2 era words per 500 words, or 2 in one paragraph
- L14 Trailing -ing Gloss: ", ensuring alignment and fostering..." clauses that claim significance
- L16 Copula Dodge: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" where "is" or "has" is meant

Full examples and fixes: [language/modifiers.md](language/modifiers.md)

## Information voids (L2, L5, L10, L11, L15, L17)

Sentences carry zero information: openers that clear their throat, closers that say nothing, appeals to consensus that skip the argument, and significance announced instead of shown.

- L2 Fortune-Cookie Close: a dramatic ending that could close any pitch about anything
- L5 Breathless Opener: "In today's fast-paced..." throat-clearing before the point
- L10 Consensus Appeal: "Everyone knows..." to skip proving the claim, and vague authority ("experts argue", "studies show") with no named source
- L11 Temporal Urgency Fake: "Now more than ever", always true and never meaningful
- L15 Significance Inflation: "a testament to", "marks a pivotal moment", "evolving landscape"
- L17 Importance Flag: "this matters", "here's the thing", "crucially", announcing importance instead of showing it

Full examples and fixes: [language/voids.md](language/voids.md)

## Claim evasion (L4, L7, L8, L9, L12)

Output avoids any concrete, falsifiable claim through hedging, abstract verbs, false choices and hollow contrasts.

- L4 Weasel Hedge: "helps you potentially reduce some friction"
- L7 Empower/Unlock/Leverage: abstract verbs replacing literal descriptions
- L8 False Binary: "either the old way or our product", hiding all alternatives
- L9 Anthropomorphized Product: "our platform understands", when software processes
- L12 Hollow Contrast: the contrast-reframe family ("not just X but Y", "more than a X", "rather than simply"), at most one per piece

Full examples and fixes: [language/evasion.md](language/evasion.md)
